**W1: Matching the memory controls and interpreting their contribution**

We agree that inference-time resetting alone introduces a training–forecasting mismatch and does not isolate the benefit of learning with memory. Our primary comparison therefore uses a separately trained reset/reset control, with memory reset throughout both warm-up and main training as well as forecasting. The revised setup holds the training and evaluation settings listed above fixed, rather than combining models with different training horizons, learning rates, or update counts. The historical training-tail sweeps remain explicitly identified as separate supplementary studies.

For the original matched SWA comparison, carry/carry reduces ten-day GraphCast loss by 14.42%, versus 9.16% for reset/reset. The paired difference is 5.26 percentage points, with a pointwise 95% date-block-bootstrap interval of [4.70, 5.86]. At day 10, the difference is 8.02 points [7.20, 9.05]. These replace the earlier unmatched 10.77-point comparison. Carry/reset is retained as a diagnostic of dependence on learned memory, with its mismatch explicitly acknowledged.

Figure 6 also adds an independent replication in which every policy uses raw main-step-2,000 weights. The five carry/carry seeds achieve mean reductions of 13.51% over the rollout and 20.19% at day 10, versus 9.21% and 13.09% for the local reset/reset control. All five carry/carry seeds exceed this control at both endpoints. However, reset/reset still has one training seed: we do not present this as a five-seed paired estimate of the memory contribution, and we do not mix SWA carry models with raw reset controls.

**W2 and Q1: Evaluation size, training seeds, and uncertainty**

The principal evaluation now spans 122 initializations at 00 UTC every third day throughout 2023, each with 40 six-hour forecast leads. Training uses 2015–2021 and configuration selection uses the 2022 development sample. We disclose prior numerical-pilot exposure to three starts in 2023; this is not presented as a completely untouched test year. Excluding those three starts leaves the original matched memory contrast essentially unchanged: 5.27 percentage points [4.70, 5.90].

For the five new SWA retrainings, the ten-day loss reduction is 14.17% ± 0.43 percentage points, and the day-10 reduction is 21.03% ± 0.75 percentage points (mean ± sample SD). Individual results range from 13.69% to 14.84% and from 20.08% to 22.15%, respectively. Figure 4 and the appendix report all five seeds, without selecting a preferred seed. These are fresh retrainings of the fixed recipe; the original selected checkpoint is reported separately, not counted as a sixth seed. The appendix documents the reconstructed training environment and local verification-data repairs.

We distinguish variation across training seeds from uncertainty over forecast dates. The latter uses 10,000 paired moving-block-bootstrap draws with blocks of five consecutive initialization positions. Figures 5 and 9 preserve the shared ENS-availability masks after resampling the original regular date grid. Their intervals concern fixed checkpoints; they do not establish training-seed robustness or simultaneous significance across all diagnostics and leads. Five seeds strengthen repeatability evidence for correction skill, while reset-control seed variation and between-year robustness remain limitations.

**Q2: Mamba versus other temporal mechanisms**

Our central claim concerns the benefit of persistent temporal conditioning in a correction branch, rather than Mamba's superiority over GRU, LSTM, or fixed-history alternatives. The matched reset/reset control holds architecture and parameter count fixed, directly addressing whether retained state helps this architecture.

Mamba was motivated by its selective state-space formulation and computational design, including a hardware-aware scan for sequence processing and efficient recurrent inference [1]. These properties do not establish greater expressive power or better weather-forecast accuracy than parameter-matched GRU/LSTM alternatives. In our autoregressive pipeline, GraphCast computation dominates, and forecast feedback restricts temporal parallelism; we therefore do not claim an end-to-end speed advantage from Mamba here. We have not added GRU/LSTM or fixed-history comparisons. Such comparisons would be needed to establish whether Mamba is preferable to other temporal corrections, a question beyond the memory-versus-reset evidence reported in this revision.

[1] Albert Gu and Tri Dao. Mamba: Linear-Time Sequence Modeling with Selective State Spaces. arXiv:2312.00752, 2023.
