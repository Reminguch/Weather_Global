Training uses ERA5 2015-2021, model selection uses 32 initializations from 2022, and principal testing uses 122 initializations spanning 2023. In the independent replication using raw update-2,000 checkpoints for both policies, all five M1 retrainings outperform the local R1 retraining. Their mean rollout loss reduction is 13.51%, with a sample standard deviation of 0.42 percentage points, compared with 9.21% for R1. Q2. How does the proposed method compare to GRU/LSTM or fixed history correction models that match parameters? This comparison will clarify whether these gains come from Mamba specific memory mechanisms or more general temporal conditioning effects.

Response:

We thank the reviewer for raising this distinction. Our experiment tests whether retaining temporal state improves correction skill within a fixed architecture and parameter count. M1 and R1 share the same Mamba-based branch and differ in their temporal-state policy during training and forecasting. This comparison supports a benefit from persistent state in that branch, but cannot attribute it to a mechanism unique to Mamba.

We have not performed the parameter-matched GRU/LSTM or fixed-history correction experiments needed to establish whether Mamba has an advantage over other ways of incorporating temporal context. We have no a priori expectation that Mamba offers greater expressive power or forecasting accuracy than suitably designed GRU/LSTM corrections in this setting.

We chose Mamba for its selective state-space formulation and computational design, including hardware-aware parallel scanning and efficient recurrent inference ([Gu and Dao, arXiv:2312.00752](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00752)). In our pipeline, autoregressive feedback makes forecast steps sequential, so we do not exploit Mamba's parallel scan across those steps. Repeated GraphCast computation dominates the cost, and we do not claim a measured end-to-end speed advantage here.
