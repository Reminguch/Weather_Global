Q1. Could the authors report energy spectra of the forecasts at long lead times to show whether the RMSE gains come from smoother forecasts?

Response:

We have added day-5 and day-10 amplitude and coherence diagnostics to distinguish attenuation from spatial-pattern agreement, together with a fitted output-filtering control.

The filter fits a separate spherical-harmonic gain for each lead, variable/pressure level, and total wavenumber by minimizing coefficient-domain squared error on 32 development initializations from 2022. The gains are frozen before evaluation and applied only to GraphCast's reported outputs, leaving autoregressive feedback unchanged. Raw GraphCast, filtered GraphCast, M1, and the matched reset-trained control R1 are evaluated on the same 122 initializations from 2023 and 40 six-hour leads.

Over the full ten-day rollout, M1 achieves 4.02% lower GraphCast-weighted MSE than filtered GraphCast and lower aggregate power mismatch (67.56% versus 78.40%); raw GraphCast has lower mismatch than either (59.47%). Thus, the tested output filter does not reproduce M1's joint accuracy and spectral-fidelity performance, although smoothing may still contribute to its gains. The mismatch metric averages absolute relative-power errors equally over leads, channels, and wavenumbers; it is not a percentage of atmospheric energy lost. Table 6 reports both errors as ratios to raw GraphCast.
