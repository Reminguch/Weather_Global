Spectral evidence: proposed manuscript presentation
Recorded 2026-11-13 (America/New_York)

Purpose
Show whether M1's forecast-error advantage survives independently fitted output
filtering, then distinguish spectral amplitude fidelity from pattern coherence.
Use established diagnostics from GraphCast and AMSE. Figures use our own data;
original AMSE figures are reference material, not additional model evaluations.

Evidence used
The new comparison uses the same 92 completed 2023 starts for every model and
lead, with filters fitted independently on 32 starts in 2022. It is an interim
subset of the planned 122 dates, not a completed annual result. Models are the
public GraphCast_small backbone and the original selected M1 checkpoint, with
raw and optimally filtered outputs. This is not a five-seed spectral experiment.
The original GC/M1 figures reused saved outputs. The later requested matched
reset/reset addition needs a new spectral evaluation because its original
122-date evaluation saved only scalar metrics, not fields or spectral moments.
Use the same 92 dates and original matched reset SWA, with no training or
filter fitting. See ../experiments/v24_matched_reset_spectra_2026-11-13.md.
Existing spectral point estimates have no confidence
bands; the seed and date uncertainty bands in other manuscript figures must
not be transferred to these spectral plots.

Follow AMSE Fig.3 using forecast/truth amplitude ratio and spectral coherence

Plot the original values without offsets. Ratios are computed

Question: are amplitude and spatial-pattern agreement changing together?

Matched reset/reset addition to every figure
Use the separately trained R_matched SWA, not inference-reset M1 or the older
unmatched reset control. The exact-loss plot already uses its archived losses
on the same 92 dates. The six spectral figures await raw spectral moments and
will regenerate automatically after that evaluation. Include raw reset as a

figure; no optimally filtered reset is implied or evaluated. This comparison
tests a memory-dependent tradeoff; do not presume joint improvement in advance.

Appendix
Include individual Z500/T850/Q700/T2m absolute spectra and normalized amplitudes
at six hours, five days and ten days. Preserve the complete represented degree
range and any excess power. Include the full lead-dependent exact-loss curves,
filter fitting protocol, channel weights, projection convention, cohort dates,
and provenance. Do not infer a general smoothing mechanism from one aggregate.
The earlier mean-relative-power-error scatter can remain a secondary summary;
it should not be the principal spectral evidence.

Reading the AMSE comparison
AMSE Fig.3 shows improved amplitude fidelity. Figs.4/12 assess lagged-ensemble
mean RMSE, not individual-forecast MSE; their accuracy changes are mixed. At
five days Fig.12 labels roughly 5% worse Q250 RMSE, 4% worse Q850, and 6% worse
precipitation RMSE. These are rounded plot labels, not a global MSE score.
No directly comparable aggregate deterministic GraphCast-loss result was found.
Thus cite AMSE for spectral diagnostics without claiming its sharper fields
preserve or worsen our particular deterministic loss by a known percentage.

Interpretation for our paper
On the interim subset, M1's raw output has lower exact loss and lower aggregate
power mismatch than optimally filtered GC. Relative to raw GC, M1 improves
loss but worsens mean power matching. These statements do not imply improvement
at every degree or for every variable. Retain day ten as an extrapolation
diagnostic because the manuscript claims ten-day accuracy improvements.
Do not compare our 1-degree/ERA5/2023 scores numerically with AMSE's different
resolution and verification setup. Do not add excluded full-GC training arms.

The new plots retain an important numerical distinction: the filters optimize
represented spherical error, whereas the canonical score is exact native-grid
GraphCast loss. Reconstruction discards unresolved components, so filtered
native-grid loss can be worse at early leads. Keep those curves visible. At
five days raw M1 has 2.2379% lower exact loss than filtered GC; independently
filtered M1 has 1.6333% lower loss than filtered GC. These are point estimates
on the same 92 dates. The new loss plot uses the repeated paired raw GC as its
denominator, not the archived canonical v4 trajectory.
