**Competing ideas and publication venues for GraphCast–Mamba**

Search date: September 18, 2026. Scope: persistent forecast-time memory, state-space weather models, non-Markovian learned corrections, and adaptation of frozen forecasters. Venue labels below use official proceedings, publisher pages, or published-paper headers. A preprint or an under-review submission is not treated as conference acceptance. This is a targeted literature search, not a systematic review or proof of priority.

**Assessment**

The main ingredients have clear precedents. The most relevant are MemNO for the physical motivation and SSM memory, SwinRNN/SwinVRNN for persistent memory in global weather forecasts, GauMamba for recurrent Mamba weather prediction, and the Mamba–ConvLSTM SST model for persistent Mamba states across ten-day forecasts. Frozen-model correction also has separate precedents.

No identical published system combining a frozen GraphCast, a separate graph correction branch, and persistent Mamba state throughout the autoregressive forecast was identified in the sources checked. That narrower combination is a possible contribution; the search does not establish that it is first. Evidence about when and why the combination helps will matter more than the combination's name.

**Details that affect the novelty claim**

MemNO's Appendix G.1 explicitly discusses Mamba as an alternative to S4 and leaves that comparison to future work. Simply substituting Mamba for S4 is therefore an anticipated extension. [Official paper, Appendix G.1](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/89379d5fc6eb34ff98488202fb52b9d0-Paper-Conference.pdf)

GauMamba is a genuine temporal-memory precedent: each MambaGRU block consumes a previous-timestamp hidden state and emits the next hidden state. Its paper also includes memory ablations. The distinction to explain is the forecast setting, representation, memory implementation, and frozen-backbone adaptation. [Method §3.4 and ablations §4.3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14895v1)

The SST hybrid is particularly relevant to the claim of retaining Mamba state across a forecast trajectory. Its regional, single-field application and joint training leave a substantial application/architecture difference, but persistence itself has been reported. The publisher's issue 10 is May-2 under its twice-monthly issue schedule. [Paper](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1312/14/10/898), [issue calendar](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1312/14)

Even older foundations include **ConvLSTM, NIPS 2015**, for recurrent precipitation forecasting ([proceedings](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2015/hash/07563a3fe3bbe7e3ba84431ad9d055af-Abstract.html)); **Neural Closure Models for Dynamical Systems, Proceedings of the Royal Society A, 2021**, for non-Markovian neural closures ([paper and journal metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13869)); and **A Framework for Machine Learning of Model Error in Dynamical Systems, Communications of the AMS, 2022**, for memory-dependent model-error learning ([published paper](https://clima.caltech.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/essoar.10509956.1.pdf)). “Proceedings of the Royal Society A” is a journal title.

**Recommended positioning — interpretation of the literature**

Use a claim such as: “We study persistent selective state-space memory as an adaptation mechanism for a frozen pretrained graph weather forecaster, and evaluate its contribution relative to spatial correction and alternative temporal models.” The final clause should describe completed comparisons only; the current manuscript's reset study supports a narrower comparison against resetting temporal state.

For the current abstract, a supported version is: “Building on memory-based neural forecasting, we investigate whether persistent selective state-space memory in a separate graph correction branch improves a frozen pretrained global weather model beyond additional spatial correction capacity.”

The most informative additional comparisons would be a correction trained without persistent state, matched GRU/LSTM and S4 corrections, and a correction given a longer explicit input history. Exact-loss evaluation on dates reserved from selection would address the current development-set limitation. These are recommendations, not completed experiments. A forecast-horizon or resolution analysis should distinguish an architectural benefit from a demonstrated physical closure mechanism.

The paper's related-work discussion should cover at least MemNO, SwinRNN/SwinRDM, GauMamba, the persistent-Mamba SST paper, and relevant frozen-correction methods.
